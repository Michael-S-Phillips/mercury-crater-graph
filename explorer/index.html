How the graph was built

Containment. A crater whose center lies inside a crater or basin at least twice its diameter formed after it, because the larger impact would have erased an earlier crater inside its rim. Each crater's parent is the smallest feature that contains it, so the graph is a forest of trees. Rim overlaps are not ordered, because the crater catalog has no rim-arc completeness.

Position. A child's center distance in parent radii places it on the parent's peak (< 0.2), floor (0.2–0.6), or wall (≥ 0.6).

Hollows. Hollows are the georeferenced detections of Bickel et al. (2025), merged into one entry per hollow. A crater has hollows when at least one hollow assigned to it lies inside 0.9 of its radius.

Depth of origin. The deepest level a crater exposes is its excavation depth or stratigraphic uplift, whichever is larger, plus the depth of origin of the parent surface it formed on. These are first-order scalings (Croft, 1985; Cintala and Grieve, 1998).

Craters: Herrick et al. (2018). Basins: Orgel et al. (2020). Hollows: Bickel et al. (2025), georeferenced. Code and data: https://github.com/Michael-S-Phillips/mercury-crater-graph. Cite: doi:10.5281/zenodo.23779916. Basemap: MESSENGER MDIS global mosaic (Denevi et al., 2018).
